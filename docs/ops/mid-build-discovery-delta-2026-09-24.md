## Business decisions and external evidence still needed

- **Customer-facing commerce policy — PROPOSAL / EXTERNAL DEPENDENCY:** Cost the owner's suggested free postage on £100 retail orders, including the 10% affiliate discount and TropShip fee. Confirm threshold basis, dispatch wording, cancellation, returns and support promises against supplier terms and UK consumer obligations.
- **Affiliate programme terms — PARTIAL OWNER DECISION:** Invite-only approval, public application, 10% customer discount, 10% first-order/5% subsequent-order commission are proposed. Define eligible merchandise base, attribution window, code stacking, refunds, budgets, duration, termination notice and payout approval before activation.
- **Marketing eligibility — EXTERNAL EVIDENCE:** Recheck Shopify sender authentication and test headers; inspect actual checkout reminder conditions and suppression; review legacy consent evidence. Use current-form confirmed subscribers for offers and keep account/service messages separate.
- **Research evidence — PROGRESSIVE WORK:** Obtain manufacturer labels/formula revisions and qualified outcome-specific reviews product by product. Unassessed comparison products may remain visible if they are clearly neutral and do not show unapproved effectiveness scores or recommendations. Accurate identity and displayed facts remain prerequisites for products TLL sells.
- **Account-specific TropShip tariff — EXTERNAL CONFIRMATION:** The direct welcome email supersedes the generic public threshold for conservative pricing. Obtain a fresh written confirmation of fee, threshold, supported destinations and any surcharges before publishing prices.

## Operational steps the product alone cannot remove

The owner or named reviewer must approve ambiguous pack mappings and costs, supplier and customer policies, campaign economics and scientific claims; monitor exceptions and returns; and approve any customer marketing send. Tropicana, Shopify, payment and email providers supply behaviour that local code cannot establish by itself. A no-purchase test cannot prove live fulfilment.
